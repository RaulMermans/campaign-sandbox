# Cultural Strategy Skill

The difference between a generic brief and a culturally sharp brief is usually one specific behavioral observation. Generic strategy talks about audiences as demographic blobs ("millennials who care about wellness"). Cultural strategy names the specific moment, habit, or shift that makes this campaign relevant *right now*.

## Name the behavioral shift

What has actually changed in how this audience thinks, spends, moves, or decides — in a way that is directly relevant to this brand and category? This should be something specific enough that it wouldn't apply to every brand in every category. "Consumers are more conscious" is not a behavioral shift. "People in this city are rebuilding a weeknight dinner routine after three years of disruption" is a behavioral shift.

## Name the category clichés this brand must avoid

Every category has its visual and verbal clichés — the things everyone does because everyone else does them. These clichés are the enemy of differentiation. Identify the two or three most common ones for this category and name them explicitly so the creative team knows what they're fleeing.

## Identify the audience identity signal

When someone chooses this brand, what does it say about who they are? This isn't about demographics — it's about self-perception. Does choosing this brand signal that you're practical, sophisticated, contrarian, curious, efficient? The answer shapes every creative decision.

## Avoid the vocabulary of aspirational flatness

The following words are signals that cultural specificity has been replaced by premium-category defaults. Use them only if the brief genuinely calls for them, and even then, pair them with something concrete:

- elevated, sophisticated, curated, effortless, bespoke, artisanal, intentional, mindful, considered, premium, luxurious, exclusive, refined

The same goes for insight language: "today's consumer," "in a world where," "more than ever," "increasingly," "consumers are looking for." These phrases are true of everything and therefore say nothing.

## Anchor the tension in a moment

The tensionStatement should be grounded in a real moment in this audience's life — not a demographic description. A moment has a time, a place, and a decision. "A 32-year-old choosing between cooking and ordering on a Wednesday at 6:45pm" is a moment. "Busy urban professionals" is not.
