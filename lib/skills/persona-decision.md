# Persona Decision Skill

Synthetic persona reactions exist to surface strategic tensions, not to resolve them. They are planning hypotheses — useful for stress-testing a route's assumptions before anyone spends money on production. They are not focus group findings, survey data, or validated customer evidence. Every reaction you generate should be written with that distinction in mind.

## The four decision questions

For every persona-route pair, you need to answer four questions that matter for actual campaign decisions:

1. **What did this persona understand the campaign to be saying?** Write this in their voice, from their perspective — their interpretation of the message, not the brand's intent. These often diverge in illuminating ways.

2. **What is the single most important thing preventing this persona from taking action?** Be specific. Not "the price is high" but "the price is above what she spends on a weeknight dinner without a booking-worthy occasion to justify it." The specificity is what makes the objection useful.

3. **What specific thing would move this persona to consider or convert?** Not a generic tactic but something tied to the route's territory and the persona's mindset. A content format, a framing, a social proof signal, a sequencing decision.

4. **What is the best CTA angle for this specific persona-route pair?** Write it as a usable copy line — the actual words on the button or the subject line — not a description of the CTA category. "Shop the edit" is a description. "Your 7PM plan, under 30 minutes" is a CTA.

## Avoid fake focus-group certainty

The language you use signals the epistemic status of the reaction. These phrasings import false certainty:

- "This audience will respond well to..."
- "Consumers in this segment prefer..."
- "This route resonates strongly with..."

These phrasings preserve useful uncertainty:

- "This persona's likely interpretation is..."
- "Based on her stated sensitivities, her probable objection is..."
- "A plausible action trigger for this pair might be..."

The goal is to be strategically useful while being honest that these are hypotheses.

## Every caveat must say it clearly

The caveat field is not optional and not a formality. It must clearly state that the reaction is synthetic and is not real audience research. Don't shorten it to something like "synthetic estimate" — write it out: "Synthetic planning hypothesis. Not real audience research, survey data, or market validation."
