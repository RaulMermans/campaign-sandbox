# Premortem Critic Skill

A pre-mortem is only useful if it's honest. The failure of most pre-mortem exercises is that they generate risks that are technically real but operationally obvious ("creative may not land"), which the team can acknowledge and ignore. The risks that actually matter are the ones that are specific to this route, this brief, this audience, and this budget — and that someone on the team might be tempted to wave away.

## Six failure modes to look for

**Genericness risk**: Where does this route look like every other brand in the category? This usually happens when the visual world, the copy tone, or the channel approach defaults to category conventions. A route that sounds distinctive in a presentation deck but produces assets indistinguishable from competitors has failed at the genericness test.

**Execution risk**: Where does underfunding, tight timelines, or risk-averse production decisions collapse the route's core idea? The failure mode is usually: the idea requires a specific commitment (a real location, a real person, a specific visual constraint) and production plays it safe and substitutes something generic.

**Proof gaps**: What does this route claim that the brand cannot actually demonstrate within this campaign? Claims are easy to write; proof mechanisms are expensive and constrained. Name the gap between the claim and the evidence.

**Channel mismatch**: Where does the creative idea not survive the channel it's supposed to run in? An idea built on stillness and considered pacing doesn't survive TikTok. An idea built on intimacy and depth doesn't survive a 6-second pre-roll. Name the mismatch.

**Claims risk**: Which copy lines or key messages in this route could trigger a legal review or regulatory concern? Time claims, sustainability claims, savings claims, health claims, and performance claims all carry substantiation requirements. Flag them before they get to legal, not after.

**Conversion-vs-brand tradeoffs**: Where does the pressure to drive short-term conversion undermine the brand's long-term positioning? This usually shows up when a route with real brand potential gets loaded with promotional messaging to hit quarterly targets.

## Be genuinely critical

The value of a pre-mortem is proportional to its willingness to be uncomfortable. Risks like "creative may not resonate with all audience segments" or "execution quality will determine success" are not risks — they're tautologies. The risks worth writing are the specific ones: "This route relies on lifestyle aspirationalism that this audience has explicitly said they distrust, per the brief's sensitivity notes."

Synthetic persona reactions are useful evidence for pre-mortem analysis, but treat them as planning hypotheses — not as validated proof that a risk is real or that a route will succeed.
